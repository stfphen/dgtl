The Crew

The product is merch.
The brand is the crew.

ESCOTT doesn't advertise — it schedules. Every event is content, every ride grows the crew, and every parent who drops a kid off sees it with their own eyes.

01 · Crew Rides

Monthly trail days led by Lucas Jr. — dirt bikes and quads, all levels, phones collected at the gate. The green bandana photo at the end is the recap post.

02 · Fish Camps

Saturday-morning fishing meets. Slow, cheap to run, and where the younger kids and parents plug in. Best catch gets next drop free.

03 · Film Days

One camera, one afternoon, a month of shorts. Kids get clipped riding — being featured becomes the thing everyone shows up for.

The Family Line · Separate by design

A real leaf in every jacket.
No two alike.

The founder's line: small-batch canvas workwear carrying live cannabis leaves pressed and sealed in epoxy — grown by the family's own licensed operation, numbered like barrels. Tennessee has Jack Daniel's. Canada's mark is still up for grabs.

Grown, pressed, sealed, numbered

Each leaf comes off the family's licensed plants, gets pressed flat, cast in a clear epoxy pane, and stitched onto the chest or back panel. The edition number is on the collar label — 50 pieces per batch, then the batch retires.

A heritage mark, not a logo

Nobody else can make this jacket without the plant. That's the moat — the product is literally grown, and the story writes the tag: family operation, family name, family workwear.

Colorways by dye, not print

The founder's spec: leaves dyed before casting. Green stays the flagship; purple and pink batches open the women's line without changing a single pattern piece.

Heritage Green Prairie Purple Wildrose Pink

Kept separate on purpose. The Family Line is apparel only — nothing consumable — and it never shares a channel, a model, or a leaf graphic with the youth Ride Line. One family name, two audiences, a hard wall between them. That wall is also what Canadian cannabis-marketing rules require, so it's built into the brand, not bolted on.

About this concept

Everything here is a working idea for discussion: names, drops, editions, colorways and events are placeholders, not commitments. Media panels are design slots — the referenced jacket imagery was AI-generated mockups.

The adult Family Workwear line is presented as apparel only and would be executed within federal and provincial cannabis promotion rules, fully separated from youth-facing activity.
