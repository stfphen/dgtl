The educator who turned craft into an audience.

Peter McKinnon didn't chase a trend to six million subscribers — he taught. Inside the value of teaching-first creators, and why they anchor the DGTL Influence network.

There's a version of the creator economy that runs entirely on novelty — the trend, the sound, the format of the week. Peter McKinnon built the opposite. For the better part of a decade he pointed a camera at his own process and explained it, frame by frame, until an audience of more than six million people learned to see the way he does. That is a different kind of asset than reach. It's trust — and trust is the thing brands can't buy in bulk.

It's the reason a teaching-first creator matters more to a campaign than a raw follower count suggests. When someone has spent years showing an audience how the work is made, a recommendation from them doesn't read as an ad. It reads as continuity. The DGTL Influence thesis starts here: the most valuable creators aren't the loudest — they're the ones an audience already believes.

Brands the DGTL Influence network has worked with

The value isn't the audience. It's the audience's attention.

Follower counts are a vanity metric until you ask what the audience actually does when the creator speaks. Teaching content is unusually sticky: viewers arrive to learn a skill, stay for the personality, and return on a schedule. That produces the two things a brand really pays for — depth of attention and durability. A tutorial from three years ago still ranks, still converts, still sends a stream of warm viewers toward whatever gear or software it featured.

Reach tells you how many people could see it. Trust tells you how many will act on it.

— The DGTL Influence view on creator value

This is why matching matters more than size. A camera educator carries enormous weight with a photography brand and almost none with a fast-fashion label. The skill isn't finding the biggest account — it's finding the account whose audience overlaps your buyer and whose voice your buyer already trusts. Get that right and the content stops feeling like advertising.

Because DGTL runs strategy, production, and campaign delivery under one roof, the creator match isn't a handoff to a separate media buyer — it's one team accountable end to end.

Why teaching-first creators anchor a network
  • Their audiences arrive with intent — they're already in a buying mindset for the category.
  • Evergreen content keeps performing, so a single collaboration pays out for years.
  • A recommendation reads as expertise, not endorsement — which is what makes it convert.
  • Vetted and on-brand beats big and generic every time the goal is real numbers.

None of this is unique to one creator. It's the pattern the whole roster is selected for — and the reason a brand launching into an unfamiliar audience is usually better served by the right 200,000 than the wrong two million.
