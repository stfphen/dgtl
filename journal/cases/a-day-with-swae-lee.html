One day with Swae Lee. A whole campaign in the can.

From a hotel-lobby pickup to the encore at Cabana & Rebel Toronto, DGTL embedded with the artist for a single day — and walked away with a recap film and a press-grade gallery.

The economics of artist content usually fight the art. Book too little time and you get a thin highlight; book too much and the budget balloons before a single frame is cut. DGTL's answer is a format built around a constraint: one day, run properly, from the moment the artist steps out of the hotel to the last note of the encore.

With Swae Lee, that day ran across Toronto and closed at Cabana & Rebel. The crew embedded from the lobby pickup onward — the quiet moments, the movement between rooms, the build to the stage — so the story wasn't just the show, it was the day around it. That's the difference between footage and a film.

One production block, a campaign's worth of assets. That's the whole idea.

— The DGTL artist-embed model

Why one well-run day beats a week of scattered shoots

A single continuous embed keeps the look, the crew and the story consistent — no re-lighting a "vibe" three weeks later, no stitching mismatched days in the edit. It also changes the math. When one shoot feeds a long-form recap, a run of social cuts, and a press-grade stills gallery, the cost per usable asset drops sharply while the quality holds.

What one day produced
  • A recap film — the day's arc, cut for story rather than just highlights.
  • A press-grade photo gallery — publication-ready stills for press and socials.
  • A consistent look across every asset, because it all came from one block.

It's the same principle DGTL applies from hospitality shoots to product launches: capture the feeling once, at full quality, then cut it for every place the audience lives. The artist gets a story worth keeping; the campaign gets a library instead of a clip.
