The night Sorisa first took the stage.

September 23, 2025: Dom Corleo took over Lee's Palace, and a 16-year-old Sorisa played his first-ever live set. DGTL was in the room — Freem on the bill, creators Yves and Wake Taylor in the crowd, and a breakout that hadn't happened yet.

Before the Atlantic Records deal, before Rolling Loud, before a free Lee's Palace show sold out the room in ten minutes — there was a Tuesday night in September, a rage show, and a 16-year-old stepping onto a stage for the first time. DGTL was there with a camera.

On September 23, 2025, Dom Corleo took over Lee's Palace on Bloor Street — the 500-capacity Toronto room that has launched more careers than most arenas. The bill ran deep into the city's underground: Toronto's Freem among the performers, others filling out the night, and a name barely anyone in the room knew yet — Sorisa, playing what, by DGTL's on-the-ground coverage, was his first-ever live set.

You can't manufacture a "before." You can only be in the room when it happens.

— The DGTL Influence view

The room read the future before the algorithm did

Toronto's scene has a way of knowing first. In the crowd that night were creators Yves (@luv.yves) and Wake Taylor (@wake.taylor) — two of the city's most-followed young voices — and DGTL photographed them too. The night was a snapshot of a specific Toronto moment: the rage and hyperpop wave cresting, the underground and the influencer worlds standing in the same 500-cap room, and an artist about to break clean out of it.

What the archive holds
  • Sorisa's first-ever live set — mid-performance, before the breakout.
  • The Dom Corleo takeover and Freem on a stacked Toronto bill.
  • Creators Yves and Wake Taylor in the crowd, shot the same night.
  • The 500-cap room Sorisa would return to sell out as a headliner.

Weeks later, the same stage — as the headliner

The story didn't take long to turn. That November, Sorisa came back to Lee's Palace — this time as the headliner. The free show filled the 500-person venue within ten minutes. The kid on the September undercard was, by his own next visit, the reason the line went around the block. From there it was Atlantic Records, The Feldman Agency, a Billboard Canada Artist to Watch nod, and a Rolling Loud debut in Orlando.

That's what makes the September frames matter. Anyone can cover a headliner. DGTL's archive caught the moment before — the version of the story you can't shoot in hindsight, and the reason the agency's model is to be in the room early, not after the numbers arrive.
