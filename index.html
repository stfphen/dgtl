Why we're building it

This is not a payments company. It is how DGTL keeps creators.

Worth saying plainly, because it changes what you should expect from us. DGTL Pay charges no take rate and is not built to. Creators are the scarce input to a creator agency, and how fast and how visibly they get paid is one of the few things an agency can be genuinely better at. That is the whole thesis, and it is the only thing this project is measured on.

What we are not claiming

No take rate on your settlement. No fee schedule to grow into. No forecast of what the rail earns, because it is not built to earn — it is funded as an internal programme with a budget, the way any agency funds the tools that make it better at its job.

What we actually measure

Whether creators reply to our briefs faster, book with us again more often, and stay on the roster longer than before the rail existed. Those numbers get a baseline recorded before a line of it ships, and they are the ones that decide whether the project continues.

The rule that keeps it honest

Every proposed addition answers one question first: does it move the creator supply number? If it moves settled volume, partner count or holder count without moving reply rate, repeat bookings or retention, it does not get built — however reasonable it sounds. That rule is the entire defence against this quietly becoming something else.

Phasing

Four phases. Each one has a numeric door.

No phase begins because the previous one felt done. Every gate is a number, published in advance. Phase 2 does not begin until Phase 1's gate is met — and Phase 1 does not get engineered until the evidence phase below clears.

Before anything is built: capture the baseline the project will be judged against, interview ten creators about briefs they actually took or declined, price the boring alternatives, name the Tier-1 markets, and put the arbiter question to counsel. Nothing is engineered in this phase, deliberately.

The founding constraint

Non-custodial, by architecture — not by policy.

Almost every serious risk in cross-border settlement descends from one behaviour: holding someone else's money in transit. So DGTL Pay doesn't. The money path is brand wallet → escrow contract → creator wallet. DGTL sits beside it.

Escrow release is multi-signature — brand, creator, and DGTL as tie-breaker. That makes DGTL an arbiter, not a custodian. Whether a regulator agrees is the single question this architecture rests on, which is why it goes to counsel in Phase 0 rather than after a contract is written.

What DGTL does

  • Originates the invoice and defines the escrow terms
  • Verifies deliverables against the agreed brief
  • Reconciles the ledger and exports the reporting
  • Acts as tie-breaker if a release is disputed

What DGTL never does

  • Hold the brand's funds
  • Hold the creator's funds
  • Convert fiat
  • Control the escrow release key alone
  • Take a percentage of the settlement

What that buys

  • Fiat ramps are a licensed partner's product, surfaced inside DGTL Pay
  • The perimeter question becomes "which ramp partner covers this corridor", not "which licences in forty countries"
  • Funds cannot be trapped by DGTL, because DGTL never has them

The cost is honest: creators need a wallet, and that is the entire UX problem of Phase 1. It gets solved with embedded wallets and email-based recovery, tested on three real creators before the escrow contract is written — not by holding funds to smooth onboarding.

The database test

Most "token utility" is a database row wearing a costume.

If a feature can be a row in a database, we build the row — not a token.

Every proposed use of $DGTL had to answer one question: could this just be a row in our database? If yes, it ships on the rail in Phase 1 — free, instant, and reversible if the tiering is wrong. Only the uses a database genuinely cannot serve are left to a token. Twelve went in. Five came out.

Failed the test — ships as a database row, no token

  • Fee rebates for high-volume brands — a discount tier is a row
  • Studio booking priority — a queue rank is a row
  • Creator loyalty points — points are a row
  • Membership tiers — a flag on an account is a row

Survived — a database genuinely cannot do these

  • Portable balance — value a creator keeps after leaving the platform. A database balance dies with the account.
  • Cross-org redemption — credit redeemable across DGTL and partner brands. Cross-org settlement of a private ledger does not work.
  • Partner-held balances — an agency holds value without a DGTL account. An account is required by definition.
  • Creator-fund governance — external contributors vote on grant allocation. Non-account-holders cannot vote in our database.
  • Programmable campaign incentives — third parties build on the primitive. A closed database is not composable.

All five survivors share one property: a party outside DGTL's account system needs to hold or act on value. That property is what a bearer token is actually for, and it is the honest answer to "why does this need a blockchain." The list is closed — it does not get extended for marketing reasons.

Token status

$DGTL — status: not minted

  • There is no supply. No contract has been deployed to any mainnet.
  • There is no price, and none will be published before a market exists.
  • There is no sale of any kind — no public offering, no private round, no SAFT.
  • There are no allocation promises to anyone, including waitlist members and creators working with DGTL today.
  • When distribution happens, it happens against verified platform activity only. Every token in circulation will have been earned by doing something on the platform.
  • Supply will be fixed at 1,000,000,000 $DGTL with no discretionary mint. Mint authority is renounced or permanently multisig-locked at deployment.
  • Redemption against services is the sink: tokens burn or return to the earn pool. A token with sources and no sinks only moves one direction.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

What does this cost to run, and who pays for it?

DGTL pays for it, out of an internal budget, the way an agency funds any tool that makes it better at its job. There is no take rate on your settlement and no fee schedule to grow into.

The honest constraint is the recurring cost, not the build. A build is a one-off. Counsel retainers, contract re-audits, ramp-partner minimums, monitoring and dispute handling arrive every month whether the project is working or not. So the project carries a stated ceiling: if the estimated ongoing run-rate exceeds five thousand dollars a month before a single external creator is onboarded, it pauses and goes back to the board. That number is published here on purpose — a project that cannot say what would stop it is a project nobody can evaluate.

Who owns the build?

As of today, nobody — and that is recorded rather than glossed over. DGTL is concurrently building a CRM, a dashboard and a terminal, and adding a settlement product to that list without naming an owner means the owner is whoever is left at the end of the week.

So Phase 0 is deliberately engineering-free: six weeks of interviews, arithmetic and one counsel call, work that runs alongside existing commitments. Naming a Phase 1 engineering owner who is not also delivering the CRM is one of the four conditions in the Phase 0 gate. If that person does not exist and cannot be hired or contracted, Phase 1 does not start — regardless of what the other three conditions say.

What happens if the Phase 0 gate fails?

The rail does not get built, and the project stops. That is the point of running the evidence phase before the engineering phase rather than after it.

Two of the four conditions are designed to be genuinely failable. The creator-interview condition asks for a specific past brief where payment terms changed what someone did — not whether faster payment sounds appealing, because that question has never once been answered no. And the alternatives condition asks whether simply paying creators faster out of working capital, or asking a brand to pre-fund an ordinary invoice, buys the same result. If either does, there is no reason to build anything, and six weeks of asking questions is the entire loss.

Is DGTL holding my money at any point?

No. The brand funds escrow from its own wallet and the creator is paid directly to theirs. DGTL is a signer on the release and an arbiter in a dispute, never a custodian of the balance. The figure is zero, and it is zero because of how the system is built rather than because of a policy that could change.

Fiat on and off ramps are a licensed partner's product in each corridor, under their licence and their KYC — not DGTL's.

Do I need to touch crypto to work with DGTL?

You need a wallet to be paid on the rail, and that is the honest cost. It is an embedded wallet with email-based recovery, not a seed phrase written on paper, and it gets tested with real creators before the escrow contract is written.

You never need to hold, buy or understand $DGTL. It does not exist, it is not required to use DGTL Pay, and it never will be.

Can I buy $DGTL, or get an allocation for joining early?

No, on both counts. $DGTL has not been minted, issued, offered or sold, and there is no mechanism by which anyone can acquire it today. Joining the waitlist carries no allocation, no entitlement and no promise of one — for waitlist members, for creators currently working with DGTL, or for anyone else.

If the token ever activates in Phase 3, distribution happens against verified platform activity only. Every token in circulation would have been earned by doing something on the platform.

Why should I believe the phase gates are real?

Because they are numeric, published in advance, and several of them are conditions this project can fail. The Phase 1 gate does not measure settled volume — DGTL could route its own campaigns through the rail, clear a large number, and prove nothing. It measures whether creators reply faster, book again, and stay.

The project also publishes what would kill it: cost per retained creator running above what it costs to source a new one for two consecutive quarters, a run-rate above the stated ceiling, a custody determination from counsel, or disputes above ten percent of campaigns. A separate, shorter list would end the token and keep the rail.
